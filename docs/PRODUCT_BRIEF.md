# Good Company

## Product Brief v0.1

**Status:** Companion to Build Plan v0.1

**Product direction:** Story-driven AI work, learning, and professional simulation

**Reference scenario:** *The Dashboard Nobody Needed*

**Primary validation goal:** Determine whether people move from understanding a governed AI collaboration pattern to applying it to consequential work of their own.

---

# 1. Product Thesis

Good Company teaches people how to work with AI through stories they can watch, enter, remix, and eventually create themselves.

It does not begin by explaining AI. It shows a recognizable ensemble solving a recognizable problem, makes the reasoning and authority visible, and then invites the user into the same Room.

The simplest expression is:

> **See what is possible. Step into the story. Build your own Good Company.**

The product experience moves through three stages:

**Watchable → Playable → Ownable**

---

# 2. The Problem

Most AI products expose capability through a blank text box. They assume the user already knows:

- What AI can do.
- How to frame a problem.
- What good collaboration looks like.
- When to use a specialist.
- What evidence to request.
- Where human judgment belongs.
- How to evaluate the result.
- How to turn one successful interaction into a repeatable process.

That creates a large cognitive and operational burden. Users may receive fluent output without understanding whether the problem was framed correctly, the evidence is sufficient, the right specialist was involved, or a human decision is still required.

Good Company replaces the blank-box starting point with a visible play pattern:

1. A story demonstrates the method.
2. Characters make responsibilities recognizable.
3. A scenario gives those responsibilities a consequential problem.
4. An interactive case lets the user practice the method.
5. A Room transfers the method to real work.

---

# 3. Target Users

The initial users are people making consequential product and operational decisions with AI:

- Founders.
- Product leaders.
- Operators.
- AI-heavy builders.
- Innovation leaders.

They have a real decision or build under consideration and need better framing, evidence, challenge, coordination, and implementation readiness.

V0.1 does not assume that these users understand agent architecture, prompt engineering, model routing, or AI governance terminology.

---

# 4. Desired Progress

The user should move from:

> “I have an idea and AI can build it quickly.”

to:

> “I understand the human problem, the supporting evidence, the consequential assumptions, the decision I own, and the contract an implementation system can safely execute.”

The operating journey is:

~~~text
Raw Request
↓
Intent
↓
Evidence and Unknowns
↓
Independent Challenge
↓
Human Decision
↓
System Design
↓
Implementation Handoff
↓
Observed Outcome
↓
Corrective Learning
~~~

---

# 5. Product Promise

Good Company is a story-driven AI work and learning environment where people:

- Watch specialized digital colleagues solve real problems.
- Enter interactive versions of those scenarios.
- Apply the same governed method to their own work.
- Build reusable colleagues, workflows, and Company Knowledge.
- Learn from verified outcomes rather than output alone.

The characters are not decorative personas. Each character is a human-readable identity for a governed professional responsibility, skill set, authority boundary, and review relationship.

---

# 6. Experience Model

## Watchable

The user watches Mira, Echo, Rook, Atlas, Forge, and Nexus work through a real scenario.

The story makes visible:

- The questions.
- The evidence.
- The unknowns.
- Productive disagreement.
- Specialist responsibility.
- Human authority.
- What gets built, held, rejected, researched, or reframed.
- What happened after the decision.

## Playable

The user enters an interactive version of the same scenario.

They can:

- Choose who enters the Room.
- Ask a colleague for help.
- Inspect the evidence.
- Identify assumptions and unknowns.
- Make the decision.
- See how the scenario changes.
- Compare their reasoning with the Company’s reasoning.

## Ownable

The user replaces the fictional problem with a real problem and begins creating:

- Their Room.
- Their workflow.
- Their colleagues.
- Their Company Knowledge.
- Their recurring scenarios.

This is how entertainment becomes learning and learning becomes real work.

# 8. The Founding Company

| Colleague | Professional responsibility | Protects | Core question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mira | Cognitive Product Architect | The human problem | What are we actually trying to change? |
| Echo | Evidence and Learning Steward | What the Company knows | What supports that, and what actually happened? |
| Rook | Independent Product Critic | The Company from false confidence | What are we assuming, and what could go wrong? |
| Atlas | Systems Architect | Structural integrity | What must be true for this to work reliably? |
| Forge | Implementation Lead | Execution | What exactly are we building? |
| Nexus | Team Coordinator and Flow Director | Coordinated responsibility | Who needs to be in the Room next, and what happens next? |
| Human Owner | Decision Authority | Consequential judgment | Which uncertainty and risk are acceptable? |

Characters enter when their responsibility becomes necessary. The product should not lead with biographies or a catalog of agents.

---

# 9. Primary User Journey

1. The user encounters a Company Story built around recognizable tension.
2. The story demonstrates evidence, disagreement, authority, decision, execution, and outcome.
3. The user enters the matching Company Case.
4. The user makes consequential choices inside the same canonical scenario.
5. The case debrief shows what changed and why.
6. The user is invited to bring in a real problem.
7. A Company Room preserves the raw request and begins governed work.
8. The Room produces an approved decision and implementation-ready Build Plan.
9. The user returns to record the observed outcome.
10. The Company proposes corrective learning for human approval.

The key transfer is:

**Company Story → Company Case → Company Room**

# 10. Reference Scenario

## The Dashboard Nobody Needed

Initial request:

> “Build a customer health dashboard with red-yellow-green scores, trend charts, filters, and export before Friday.”

The ordinary response is to ask Forge to build it quickly.

Nexus pauses the work because the problem has not been defined. Mira asks what decision the customer will make with the dashboard. Echo finds that the request rests on internal assumptions rather than customer evidence. Rook challenges whether the team is building a dashboard because users need it or because dashboards look like software.

The Human Owner approves a reframe:

- Do not build a general dashboard.
- Create an exception-based Account Attention Queue.
- Show only accounts requiring review.
- Include the evidence, confidence, and freshness behind each flag.
- Keep approval of the next action with the user.

Atlas defines the states and data requirements. Forge builds the smaller system. Echo later compares predicted and observed outcomes. The Company records what was supported, contradicted, and still unknown.

This scenario is the reference case because it exercises:

- Problem framing.
- Specialization.
- Productive disagreement.
- Evidence and uncertainty.
- Human authority.
- Controlled execution.
- Outcome verification.
- Corrective learning.

The canonical case record is explicitly fictional. Its outcomes demonstrate the data and learning model but do not constitute product-validation evidence.

# 11. V0.1 Product Experiment

The product must support three controlled experimental arms:

| Arm | Experience | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | One general AI assistant | Establish ordinary AI-assisted performance. |
| CPE | Governed workflow with professional role labels | Test the reasoning and responsibility system. |
| Good Company | The identical CPE workflow with the founding cast | Test the incremental value of character and ensemble presentation. |

Between the CPE and Good Company arms, character presentation may change:

- Names.
- Portraits.
- Accent colors.
- Communication style.
- Entrance and relationship cues.

It must not change:

- Evidence standards.
- Authority.
- Workflow.
- Permissions.
- Decision rules.
- Evaluation criteria.

---

# 12. Governance and Evidence

AI colleagues may:

- Ask.
- Organize.
- Retrieve.
- Analyze.
- Compare.
- Recommend.
- Draft.
- Challenge.
- Produce implementation contracts.

The Human Owner must approve:

- Material assumptions.
- Build, Research, Hold, Reject, or Reframe decisions.
- Consequential scope changes.
- External irreversible action.
- Final output.
- Consequential Company Knowledge updates.

Material claims must remain traceable to evidence, source quality, confidence, missing evidence, contradictory evidence, and verification status.

Failure to retrieve evidence must be represented as inability to observe, not evidence of absence.

---

# 13. Required V0.1 Capabilities

- Preserve the original request unchanged.
- Establish the Human Owner and decision authority.
- Separate evidence, claims, assumptions, unknowns, and contradictions.
- Run an independent critic review.
- Present an explicit Decision Board.
- Record the Human Owner’s decision.
- Produce system boundaries, states, permissions, and acceptance criteria.
- Export an implementation-ready Build Plan.
- Capture expected and observed outcomes.
- Propose corrective learning for human approval.
- Render one canonical scenario as a Story, Case, and Room template.
- Record the assigned experimental arm and validation observations.

# 14. Primary Measures

The most important measure is not whether users like the story.

It is:

> **Do users move from watching the story to bringing a real problem into a Room?**

Core measures include:

- Story → Case entry.
- Case completion.
- Case → Real Room transfer.
- Problem-definition change.
- Consequential assumptions surfaced.
- Evidence gaps identified.
- Scope and architecture changes.
- Responsibility comprehension.
- Human decision comprehension.
- Implementation readiness.
- Clarification burden and rework.
- Observed outcome quality.
- Voluntary return with another project.

No measurements found. Synthetic or fictional records may verify the harness but may not support product conclusions.

---

# 15. Success, Failure, and Kill Signals

## Success signals

- Users identify the real problem before implementation.
- Material assumptions surface early.
- Build contracts require less clarification.
- Users distinguish recommendation, execution, and approval.
- The character layer improves responsibility comprehension or return behavior without reducing decision quality.
- Users voluntarily bring additional real work into Good Company.

## Failure signals

- CPE adds burden without improving decisions or reducing rework.
- Outputs remain too abstract for implementation.
- Evidence requirements are not proportional to consequence.
- Character identity distracts or creates false trust.
- Users remember names but not responsibilities.
- Users prefer ordinary AI assistance.

## Kill criteria

- Stop character development if repeated evidence shows no meaningful comprehension, adoption, or retention benefit.
- Change CPE if it increases process burden without materially improving decisions.
- Narrow the market if repeated value appears in only one user segment.

---

# 16. V0.1 Boundaries

Do not treat the following as V0.1 requirements:

- An open character marketplace.
- Autonomous colleague creation.
- Large custom Companies.
- Advanced skill trees or experience points.
- Public scenario marketplaces.
- Automated story-video generation.
- Broad external tool execution.
- Full enterprise governance.
- Fine-tuning pipelines.

The immediate priority is a functional Room that can persist one complete governed decision. The ensemble and narrative layers should be added without weakening the operating system.
